How far is school?

From the average residential address to the farthest screened point

What changes under the consolidation scenario

In the scenario under discussion in November 2026, grades 5–6 would go to Crossett Brook districtwide and grades 7–8 would go to Harwood Union. The November 4 straw poll directed further budget modeling. It was a preliminary step in the process.

+4.9 miWaterbury Center grades 7–8, average one-way change from Crossett Brook to Harwood
+15.1 miWarren grades 5–6, average one-way change from the Warren School to Crossett Brook
6.2 miAverage valley residential route to Waitsfield Elementary, a hypothetical middle-grades site

For grades 7–8, the average increase is 4.9 miles across Waterbury and 1.3 in Duxbury. About 70% of screened Duxbury points are farther from Harwood. For grades 5–6, the average increases from the current local elementary campuses to Crossett Brook are 9.1 miles in Fayston, 9.3 in Waitsfield, 15.1 in Warren and 1.9 in Moretown. Differences use unrounded distances.

Pooling residential points across Fayston, Waitsfield, Warren and Moretown gives 6.2 miles to Waitsfield Elementary, 9.0 to Harwood and 13.5 to Crossett Brook. Waitsfield has the lowest average of the three in Fayston, Waitsfield and Warren. Moretown averages 6.2 miles to Harwood, 7.1 to Crossett Brook and 9.6 to Waitsfield. Equal town weights and a single-family/mobile-home sensitivity check preserve the pooled ranking.

All figures are one-way road miles. The median is the midpoint. About nine in ten screened routes are at or below the 90th percentile. Each maximum is calculated independently and may come from a different address.

Method and limits

  1. Start with 6,672 Vermont E911 residential site points in the six municipalities and check the origins against Vermont town polygons. The residential selection excludes camps, seasonal homes, development sites, accessory barns and buildings, dormitories and assisted-living facilities.
  2. Use E911 school points and route each origin over the OpenStreetMap road network using the OSRM driving table. The service reports road distance along its fastest modeled car route. Convert meters to statute miles using 1,609.344 meters per mile.
  3. Keep the common set of 6,488 points with usable routes, excluding origins snapped more than 200 meters from the network. One Farr Road result was set aside because an unnamed access segment generated a long highway detour whose residential access could not be verified. The reported maximum is the farthest retained modeled route.
  4. Weight each site point equally. Ordinary residential classifications can include second homes, and one point can represent multiple units. The study does not identify occupied homes or enrolled students. Bus stops, route sequencing, turnarounds, seasonal access and actual ride times need district data.

How to use this in the budget discussion

Compare the consolidation package with an existing-campus option that keeps valley grades 5–6 closer to home and retains Crossett Brook as the north-side grades 7–8 baseline. The district reported classroom capacity for a three-school valley Pre-K–6 option, with a modeled excess-spending penalty of about $165,000. That is the projected penalty for that option, separate from its full operating cost. The preliminary October consolidation estimates were about $2.14 million from closing two valley elementary schools and $1.78 million from merging the middle-school programs at Harwood. A Waitsfield middle-grades campus is a geographic scenario in this report. Its capacity, staffing and operating costs would need evaluation.

A side-by-side district comparison would pair annual staffing and benefits, facility and transportation costs, capital needs and projected tax effects with student-weighted average and 90th-percentile bus ride times by town and grade.
